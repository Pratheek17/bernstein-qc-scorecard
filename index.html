The Verdict
Summary

Blinkit beat the model. Instamart broke it. Zepto grew without the profits.

The industry analysis was excellent; the stock pick was not.

▾
1Bernstein's FY26 scale forecasts landed within single digits for both listed players — genuinely good modelling.▾

Blinkit FY26 NOV came in at ₹48,568 crore against a forecast of ₹52,000 crore (a 7% miss), with 917 million orders against 978 million forecast. Instamart's FY26 GOV of roughly ₹28,500 crore was almost exactly the ₹28,935 crore Bernstein modelled. For a report written mid-year with a proprietary dark-store database as its main edge, that is tight work. The problem was never the FY26 numbers — it was the FY27 glide path that the investment thesis depended on.

2The one contrarian bet — believe in Instamart's profitability glide path, prefer Swiggy — is the exact thing that failed.▾

Bernstein explicitly argued against consensus: the street modelled Instamart at ~70% of Blinkit's FY30 GOV but only ~15% of its EBITDA, and Bernstein said that was too bearish, modelling 65% of GOV and 30% of EBITDA instead. A year on, Instamart's NOV is 33% of Blinkit's and falling (it was ~45% a year ago), its Q2 FY27 adjusted EBITDA margin is −9.7% against a modelled FY27 path of −2.4%, and Jefferies notes the market now ascribes negative value to Instamart. Consensus won the argument.

3The structural market calls — winner-takes-most, top-3 stability, food delivery as the cash machine — largely came true.▾

"QC has scale effects, not network effects; leader profitability will be better than the rest" is exactly what happened — arguably more extreme than Bernstein expected, with Blinkit now matching Zepto and Instamart combined on order volume and being the only profitable player. The food delivery call (structural slowdown to 16–18% growth, cash machine economics) was dead on: Swiggy's FD GOV grew 17.7% in Q2 FY27 at a record 3.4% margin. The framework was right; the relative-winner weighting inside it was wrong.

The Three Players
Beat the model

Blinkit — ahead of the only above-consensus numbers in the report

Q2 FY27: NOV +79% YoY, adj. EBITDA +0.9% of NOV, 2,751 stores

▾
1Growth: Q2 FY27 NOV of ₹20,940 cr (+79% YoY) paces Bernstein's aggressive ₹94,300 cr FY27 estimate — which was 9% above consensus.▾

Bernstein's Blinkit GOV estimates sat 5–10% above street consensus for FY26–FY28, the boldest numbers in the model. Blinkit is delivering them. Revenue hit ₹19,152 cr in Q2 FY27 alone, boosted by the 3P-to-1P inventory transition that Bernstein correctly predicted would complete fast and lift take-rates from ~23% to ~93% of NOV.

2Stores: 2,751 at Sep 2026 with the Mar 2027 target raised to 3,500 — that is Bernstein's FY28 store count, a year early.▾

The model assumed 3,001 stores by end-FY27 and 3,501 by end-FY28, with build intensity "moderating materially". Instead Blinkit added 308 stores in a single quarter and pulled the 3,500 target forward to March 2027. The expansion call was directionally right but too conservative — the rare miss in Blinkit's favour.

3Profitability: +0.9% of NOV in Q2 FY27 vs a modelled +0.7% for the full year; mature cities already run above 5%.▾

Bernstein modelled breakeven "in FY27"; Blinkit got there in Q4 FY26 (+₹37 cr) and has now posted five straight quarters of profitability gains, earning ₹184 cr in Q2 FY27. Cities contributing ~70% of NOV are already adjusted-EBITDA positive. The margin build Bernstein sketched — fixed-cost absorption from sweating stores toward 2,200 orders/day, plus ad income — is playing out on script, just faster.

Broke the thesis

Instamart — the glide path that never glided

Q2 FY27: NOV +31% YoY vs modelled +83%; adj. EBITDA −9.7% vs modelled −2.4%

▾
1Growth miss: Bernstein modelled FY27 NOV of ₹39,200 cr on an 83% growth assumption; the actual run-rate is tracking roughly 30% below.▾

The model's engine was a throughput ramp — orders per store per day jumping from ~1,094 to ~1,583 in FY27 as under-utilised 4,200 sq ft stores filled up. Q2 FY27 NOV of ₹6,954 cr (+31.4% YoY) shows the ramp is not happening at anywhere near that pace. Store count (1,240 at Sep 2026 vs 1,382 modelled for end-FY27) is roughly on path; it is the demand per store that failed the model.

2Margin miss: Q2 FY27 adjusted EBITDA of −₹895 cr (−9.7%) against a modelled FY27 path of −2.4%; contribution margin −1% vs modelled +3%.▾

Bernstein's signature anti-consensus call was that Instamart's profitability was underrated. Instead losses widened year on year (from −₹778 cr) while Blinkit earned +₹184 cr in the same quarter. The ₹10,000 cr QIP that Bernstein framed as a re-rating catalyst and competitive deterrent has instead funded continued burn.

3The "65% of Blinkit by FY30" call is moving in reverse: the NOV ratio has fallen from ~45% to 33% in four quarters.▾

For the convergence call to work, Instamart had to grow faster than Blinkit from FY27. The opposite happened: Blinkit grew 79% while Instamart grew 31% in the same quarter. Bernstein's own October 2026 availability study supplies a mechanism — Instamart's 83.8% in-stock rate against Blinkit's 94.4%, with the steepest intraday decay of any platform, pointing to the demand-forecasting and replenishment gap the initiation report underweighted. Flipkart Minutes has meanwhile overtaken Instamart on dark-store count.

Half right

Zepto — the scale happened, the profits did not

FY26: revenue 2x to ₹22,623 cr; adj. EBITDA −₹5,041 cr; IPO filed

▾
1Bernstein kept Zepto unmodelled (unlisted) but bet it stays top-3 — correct: revenue doubled to ₹22,623 cr and it holds the #2 spot on most metrics.▾

Zepto's FY26 operating revenue of ₹22,623 cr (up from ₹11,110 cr) with 1,139 dark stores keeps it comfortably in the top three, ahead of Instamart on scale. Ad revenue grew 2.5x to ₹1,636 cr, validating Bernstein's thesis that advertising is the core profit lever for QC platforms.

2But "top-3 and profitable" is still distant: adjusted EBITDA loss widened to ₹5,041 cr in FY26 (net loss ₹5,905 cr).▾

The medium-term claim that all three leaders end up profitable remains unproven — Blinkit is the only one there. The per-order trend does support the direction: adjusted EBITDA loss per order improved from ₹136 to ₹79. Zepto has filed its DRHP, so FY27 will put public numbers against the rest of this scorecard.

1The predicted "QC re-rating for Swiggy over the next six quarters" became a de-rating.▾

Bernstein's re-rating catalysts were the QIP and visible progress on the profitability glide path. The QIP happened; the glide path did not. After Q2 FY27 showed Instamart losses widening to ₹895 cr, roughly ₹9,000 cr of market value was wiped out in a single session and the stock hit a record low. At current prices the market values Swiggy below the standalone worth of its food delivery business plus cash.

2The Eternal call worked for exactly the reason the Swiggy call failed: the model trusted Blinkit's execution and doubted the street's Blinkit growth numbers.▾

Eternal is up 14% since initiation at ₹363 against a ₹390 March-2027 target, with Q2 FY27 profit up 92% on Blinkit's strength. The irony: Bernstein's below-consensus caution on Zomato food delivery growth also proved right, yet the stock still outperformed because Blinkit over-delivered — the exact winner-takes-most dynamic the report described, concentrated in the one company it ranked second in preference.
